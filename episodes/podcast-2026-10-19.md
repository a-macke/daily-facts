# The Daily Facts — Monday, October 19, 2026

Good evening, and welcome to The Daily Facts. It's Monday, October nineteenth, twenty twenty-six. I'm your host, and here's a straightforward look at the day's world news. Where details are disputed or unconfirmed, we'll say so.

## Ukraine

We begin in Ukraine, where Russia carried out a large overnight strike on energy infrastructure from Sunday into Monday. According to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, sixteen ballistic missiles were fired at the Kyiv region, more than ten energy sites were hit, and about five hundred fifty thousand families across eleven regions were left without power. Ukraine's Air Force said Russia launched two hundred sixty-nine Shahed-type drones, fifty-nine of them jet-powered, and that two hundred forty-three drones and four cruise missiles were downed. The Kyiv Independent reported three people killed and fifty-three injured over the past day. Zelenskyy said there was no early warning from partners. Russia's Defense Ministry said it struck defense industry targets and a data center in the Kyiv region.

Later Monday, Reuters reported that a drone strike on a park in Nova Vodolaha, in the Kharkiv region, killed six people, including three children, and injured about fifteen.

On diplomacy, Reuters, citing three sources, reports that Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are expected to travel to Norway this week for talks on Ukraine. Two of those sources said Zelenskyy is expected to attend. The plans are described as tentative.

The Wall Street Journal reports that Vladimir Putin has proposed a three-way summit with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping at the APEC meeting in Shenzhen on November eighteenth and nineteenth. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said there have been discussions but no agreement. President Trump has not agreed, and Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, voiced opposition. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Uzbekistan, referred to "talks of a meeting" and said there is "no pause" in intelligence sharing with Ukraine. Rubio also said Ukrainian strikes in the Black Sea hit vessels carrying US-owned oil, but that this was not deliberate.

And in Lithuania, Defense Minister Kaunas said a Ukrainian drone that entered the country was diverted by Russian electronic warfare during an attack on Saint Petersburg. Ukraine apologized. Lithuanian officials said the incident is not linked to a recent Starlink arson case, and the national security commission met Monday.

## Middle East and Iran

Turning to the Middle East. In Yemen, a Houthi missile attack on a government training camp near Ataq, in Shabwa province, has seen differing casualty figures through the day. Early on, The National and other sources said at least eighteen were dead, Reuters also said at least eighteen, AP said at least twenty, and China's Xinhua reported twenty-five. By late morning, AP and Reuters cited local authorities saying fifty-three people were killed and more than two hundred wounded. Three ballistic missiles struck the training center, and the Houthis acknowledged the attack. Officials stress the figures are preliminary.

Separately, Riyadh's airport reopened Monday, according to a notice to airmen, following Houthi attacks, though Flightradar24 data showed more than eighty percent of flights cancelled. Eighteen people have died in total in those attacks, and Lufthansa has suspended service until November eighteenth.

In Iran, there are two conflicting accounts of an incident at the Konarak naval base. Iran says US forces targeted the base; according to Iran's navy, two light projectiles hit a thirty-five millimeter gun and wounded two soldiers. A US official, speaking to CNN, called Iran's claim, quote, "inaccurate, as usual." Neither account has been independently confirmed.

Also unconfirmed: Erem News, citing unnamed US diplomatic sources, reported that President Trump discussed options for striking Iran's Kharg Island. The US has not confirmed this, and Trump said on October eighth there would be no strikes before the midterms.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov visited Tehran, meeting President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. At a joint news conference, the two sides reaffirmed their partnership. Lavrov said Russia is ready to help find a diplomatic exit between Iran and the US and to restore navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, and that Russian specialists are returning to the Bushehr nuclear plant.

Meanwhile, Revolutionary Guard spokesman Mohebbi noted that seven major undersea cables run beneath the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as "reminding the enemy," and the Guard warned of a stronger response to any renewed US action. In Kenya, the energy regulator's director general told lawmakers two Kenya-bound tankers were immobilized by drones in Hormuz; the cargo is intact, but transfer could take around sixty days. And Pakistan has suspended border trade with Iran, citing violence on both sides of the border, Al Jazeera reports.

In Egypt, parliament extended a closed session on deploying forces to Saudi Arabia, with a vote now set for Tuesday. A two-thirds majority is required.

In Gaza, the Palestinian news agency WAFA said drone strikes Monday killed two children south of Khan Younis and one person in Gaza City; medical sources said a seven-year-old was killed Sunday in Maghazi. The Israeli military did not comment. The World Food Programme says funding shortfalls will cut aid recipients in Gaza from one point six million to one point one million, and in the West Bank from four hundred thousand to two hundred thousand. It is asking for four hundred fifty million dollars. In the West Bank, Israeli police arrested ten suspects over settler attacks in the Taybeh area.

## Europe

In France, unrest continued for a second night Sunday. Paris police made one hundred eight arrests, and the BHV Marais department store and at least five other shops were looted. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced localized curfews and said about eighty percent of those arrested face charges. Paris has imposed an eleven p.m. to six a.m. curfew around Place de la République and Les Halles, with rallies barred from eight p.m. Curfews for minors apply in Lille, Douai, Wattignies, Denain and central Nice, and Marseille banned rallies Monday afternoon. The interior minister said police had "lost control," and Lecornu would not rule out a state of emergency. A yellow vest "Act Two" is called for Saturday, October twenty-fourth, and unions plan strikes on November fifth.

In Germany, the government summoned Iran's ambassador after a Hamburg court jailed two men, for eight and a half and four and a half years, over an Iran-directed plot targeting Jewish community leader Josef Schuster and Volker Beck.

Storm Adam has killed five people in Spain: three in Valencia, one in a rockfall near Málaga, and one in Catalonia, with up to seven hundred millimeters of rain in forty-eight hours. In Greece, twelve migrants died in two boat incidents off Lesbos and Samos, including an infant; smuggling suspects were arrested. And in Turkey, flooding has killed eight people in Istanbul and Kocaeli, with one missing, in the heaviest rain there since nineteen forty-two.

## Markets

To markets. On Wall Street, the S and P five hundred closed up zero point four one percent at seven thousand six hundred ninety point seven three. The Nasdaq rose one point one one percent, while the Dow slipped zero point zero seven percent. The ten-year Treasury yield stood at about five point one four percent, and Brent crude was near one hundred nine dollars and fifty-five cents a barrel, according to the Wall Street Journal. In Europe, French stocks fell about one and a half percent as French bond yields rose. Indonesia's main index closed down three point six four percent after a near twelve percent drop Friday, and the exchange tightened its limit-down threshold. China reported third-quarter growth of four point five percent year on year.

## Other news

In Australia, MP Helen Haines remains in critical condition after surgery, having been struck by an SUV outside her office in Wangaratta. A forty-three-year-old man is in custody, with no charges announced yet. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said it appears deliberate; the motive is unknown.

In Kenya, a court in Mombasa found cult leader Paul Mackenzie and co-accused guilty of one hundred ninety-one murders in the Shakahola case, most of them children. Sentencing is set for November sixth.

In the United States, the Justice Department charged twenty-eight foreign nationals in California over alleged illegal voter registration and voting; California's attorney general said there is no widespread voter fraud. In Columbus, Ohio, one person was killed in a shooting near a mosque; police say it stemmed from an altercation at nearby apartments and was not an attack on the mosque.

In Canada, Albertans voted in a ten-question referendum, including one on beginning a process toward a separation vote. Official results are due October twenty-ninth.

And Japan has launched its Martian Moons eXploration mission, aiming to land on Phobos and return samples to Earth in twenty thirty-one.
