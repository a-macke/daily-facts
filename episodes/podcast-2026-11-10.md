In Washington, the House voted 211 to 204 this evening to send a resolution impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee. The vote blocks a planned vote on the charges themselves.
The resolution, filed by Republican Congressman Thomas Massie of Kentucky, contained eight articles. Most concern the war with Iran.
Earlier today, Mr. Hegseth held his first Pentagon news briefing in about six months. He called the impeachment effort a, quote, "farce," end quote, and defended his record on the Iran war.
President Trump praised Mr. Hegseth and called Mr. Massie, quote, "a total loser," end quote. After the vote, Mr. Trump said on social media that the effort had, quote, "miserably failed," end quote.
The Philadelphia Inquirer reports Pennsylvania's House delegation voted along party lines.
In leadership elections, Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune as their leader. House Republicans chose Jim Jordan to lead them in the next Congress. Senator Bernie Sanders says he will not back Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic leader, Bloomberg reports.
The Justice Department indicted three men it says were backed by Iran. Prosecutors accuse them of nearly 20 attacks or attempted attacks on Jewish sites in the United States and Europe. They face terrorism-related charges.

Turning to Iran. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said after a G 7 meeting in Paris there is, quote, "no need," end quote, for further major combat operations. He cited the American economic pressure campaign. Diplomats told Reuters he also said the United States will keep imposing, quote, "devastating" costs, end quote, on Iran.
Iran's account differs. Its top security official told Reuters no talks with the United States are underway. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi denied reports of a planned meeting between Vice President Vance and security chief Mohsen Rezaei. Tehran urged Washington to accept its demands on the Strait of Hormuz.
Canada reimposed sanctions on 11 Iranian entities.
In Yemen and Saudi Arabia, the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted two Houthi missiles fired toward Riyadh. Al Jazeera reports an oil company building in Sanaa was struck overnight, after a Houthi attack damaged an Aramco office in Riyadh.
In Iran, a fire at the South Pars gas field has been put out. Al Jazeera reports 17 workers jumped into the sea to escape.
Oil prices fell below $90 a barrel today.

Officials from Ukraine, Russia and the United States opened two days of talks in Abu Dhabi. Reuters reports they are the first such talks since February.
Agence France-Presse reports Washington called for a, quote, "new" security framework, end quote, on the first day. President Zelensky said he was, quote, "happy," end quote, with changes in the approach of the American mediators.
Secretary Rubio says the United States is closely aligned with Europe on ending the war.
As the talks opened, Russia struck Ukraine's power grid, Reuters reports. Tonight, a drone attack left part of the city of Kryvyi Rih without power.

On the economy, consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, the Labor Department reports. Economists had expected 0.3%. Core prices, which exclude food and energy, rose 0.3%, also above forecasts. Gasoline rose 1% and food rose 0.5%.
No year-over-year rate was published, because of a data gap from last year's government shutdown.
Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller backs a rate hike in December. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan says she wants at least two quarter-point increases. Reuters reports market odds of a half-point hike in December jumped.
Stocks closed lower. Treasury prices fell, and the dollar posted its strongest gain since September, Bloomberg reports.
The New York Fed says credit card balances rose $22B in the third quarter, to a record $1.29T.
