The Federal Aviation Administration this afternoon ordered a ground stop on all departing flights at American airports.
The FAA cited, quote, "extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," end quote.
The AP reports the cause is a communications problem at regional air traffic control centers. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy says the problem affected all air route traffic control centers.
CBS News reports it is the first nationwide ground stop since September 11th, 2001.
Bloomberg reports the FBI does not believe a cyberattack or criminal act was involved. Homeland Security Secretary Mullin said there is no indication of an attack.
CBS News, citing sources, reports the failure followed an update to the private network that links the air traffic control system. CBS says the update caused the system to reboot and crash. That account has not been officially confirmed.
As of this evening, no time has been given for a full resumption.
United Airlines plans to cancel most of its remaining flights tonight, with some international exceptions. JetBlue is canceling all remaining flights tonight, except Europe-bound departures from New York's JFK and Boston.
In New Orleans, a tanker struck the American Heritage, a river cruise boat docked on the Mississippi River, at about 12:30 this afternoon.
Mayor Helena Moreno says the tanker lost control. The riverboat was carrying about 129 people. It was left on its side and largely submerged.
The AP reports more than 30 people were taken to hospitals. BNO News reports no one is unaccounted for.
Microsoft says its Outlook, Teams and Copilot services are recovering from an outage that lasted more than 12 hours. Al Jazeera reports users in 74 countries were affected.
Reuters reports Microsoft blamed a faulty server and said the outage was not malicious. Microsoft later apologized and tied the outage to a network capacity upgrade. Microsoft says the outage was not related to the air traffic failure.
