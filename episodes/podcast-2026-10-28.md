## World: Spain train collision
Sources: CBS News (at least 68 killed) https://x.com/CBSNews/status/2115559505631346779 ; Reuters (at least 68) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2115463599540085127 ; AFP (64 killed, toll likely to rise) https://x.com/AFP/status/2115461429289513206 ; Reuters (second deadly crash on line) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2115417049115881840 ; Reuters (Madrid minute of silence) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2115518888503087457
In Spain, a high-speed train slammed into the rear of a stopped train this morning, near the town of Urda in Toledo province.
Two cars derailed. Service on the line between Madrid and Andalusia was suspended.
Local authorities say at least 68 people were killed, CBS News and Reuters report. AFP gave a toll of 64 earlier today, with more than 100 injured, and said the toll was likely to rise. All figures are preliminary.
Reuters reports it is the second deadly accident in less than a year on the same line.

In Israel, the vote count continues one day after the election.
With 85.6% of votes counted, Al Jazeera reports Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats. The opposition has 48, and Arab parties have 19. A majority requires 61.
Al Jazeera reports between 430,000 and 480,000 double-envelope ballots had not yet been counted as of midday.
Mr. Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot both claim victory.
This evening, Likud alleged that Arab parties stole the election through bribery, threats, violence and voter fraud. Likud says it gave hundreds of pieces of evidence to police, the Shin Bet security agency and election officials. CBS News reports Mr. Netanyahu asked the Shin Bet to investigate. Those allegations have not been independently verified.
A response from the Arab parties to the latest allegation was not included in the reports we checked.
Earlier, former Joint List leader Ayman Odeh says Mr. Netanyahu's bloc would have won outright without the votes of Palestinian citizens of Israel.
Joint List leader Yousef Jabareen says the alliance could back an anti-Netanyahu government from outside, but would not join it. Democrats leader Yair Golan says the United Arab List could join a governing coalition if the opposition removes Mr. Netanyahu.
President Isaac Herzog urged Israelis to avoid another election.
Reuters reports Mr. Netanyahu could remain caretaker prime minister during coalition talks.

On Iran, President Trump said ending the war is a matter of when he chooses.
CBS News obtained a Central Command estimate putting the cost of the war at about $47B.
US media reported that Mr. Trump directed Vice President Vance to open peace talks with Iran. Mr. Trump denied that. He said a letter in question was about medical supplies.
Iranian state media quote President Pezeshkian as saying the government is working to end a state of, quote, "neither war nor peace," end quote.

In Yemen, the Houthis struck Aden airport after issuing an evacuation warning, Reuters reports. Yemeni government forces say two ballistic missiles were fired, the sixth such attack, and that civilians were injured. No number of injured was given. Air traffic was suspended.
In Lebanon, Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem rejected calls for the group to give up its weapons. He denounced a US-backed framework agreement signed by Israel and the Lebanese government, and said it violates Lebanon's constitution.

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady today, as expected.
Reuters reports the decision puts off any change until after next week's midterm elections. CBS News notes the hold came even as inflation remains high.
Two members dissented and called for an immediate rate increase, AFP reports. Bloomberg describes a divided Fed, with no clear signal of a hike in December.
Fed Chair Warsh says the Fed will reduce the number of its rate-setting meetings, phased in starting next year.
In Canada, the central bank raised its key rate for the first time since 2023. It cited inflation risk from the oil price surge tied to the Iran war.

The World Bank expects commodity prices to rise 23% this year, citing the Middle East war and other shocks.
In company earnings, Microsoft shares rose about 5% after results beat expectations. Alphabet's core sales disappointed, and its shares fell after hours. Ford reported mixed results.
