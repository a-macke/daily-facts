## World: Iran and the Gulf

President Trump said this evening that the war with Iran will end, quote, "very soon, one way or the other," end quote. He said Tehran must make a deal, or Washington will end the war itself.
Earlier today, Reuters reports, the president said he is in no hurry on a deal. He declined to say whether fighting would resume after Tuesday's midterm elections. He says several targets remain in Iran.
Iran has a different message. Iranian officials warned today that any new war could last longer than the last one, and that President Trump would not decide how long it lasts.
Talks through Qatari mediators continued for a third day, Reuters reports. An Iranian security source told Al Jazeera the talks go on, but with deep skepticism on the Iranian side. Iran says it could reopen the Strait of Hormuz sooner if its conditions are met.
On the water, Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy says two tankers exploded after hitting mines in the strait. That claim has not been independently confirmed. Britain's maritime trade agency, U.K.M.T.O., received a single report of a vessel struck by an unknown projectile.
Gulf states condemned Friday's attack on a Qatari liquefied natural gas carrier. No one has claimed that attack.
The head of the International Maritime Organization, Arsenio Dominguez, told Bloomberg about 12 vessels passed through Hormuz in the past week. Before the war, the average was 135.
Most Gulf stock markets fell today. Qatar's index rose.

## World: Ukraine and Russia

In Ukraine, the longest truce of the war has ended. The ceasefire covered only part of the Kherson region, around the occupied town of Oleshky. Ukrainian officials say Russia refused an offer to extend it.
Ukraine began evacuating civilians from parts of the Kherson region ahead of the truce's expiry, Reuters reports.
Reuters also reports that Russian strikes cut power to thousands of Ukrainians today, and that Ukrainian drones damaged a facility in Russia.
Ukraine's air force says its public air raid alert app was hacked. It did not say who was responsible.
In Moscow, Russian news agency Interfax, citing the F.S.B. security service, says a teenager was detained on suspicion of planning an attack on a park and museum. That follows a warning from the U.S. Embassy on Saturday.
And Ghana's foreign minister says President Putin agreed to stop recruiting Ghanaians to fight for Russia.

In Bulgaria, President Iliana Iotova won a runoff vote today, according to exit polls. AFP notes she favors dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine.
In Germany, hooded rioters looted stores in Frankfurt after nationwide youth protests on Saturday night. Police used water cannon in several cities. German ministers pledged tough action.
In France, prosecutors opened an attempted murder investigation after a petrol bomb was thrown at security forces during a protest in Paris.
In Serbia, tens of thousands gathered in Novi Sad for an anniversary rally, Reuters reports.
In New Delhi, tens of thousands marched behind opposition leader Rahul Gandhi. They demand the chief election commissioner resign. Protesters allege more than 130 million names were removed from voter rolls. The Election Commission rejects that claim.
In the Democratic Republic of Congo, Bloomberg reports the death rate among confirmed Ebola cases has passed 50%. That followed the addition of hundreds of previously uncounted deaths to the official toll.
And Colombia's vice president warns the country risks default if Congress does not approve a fiscal adjustment bill.

In Florida, a Learjet 60 flying from Austin to the Bahamas crashed into a residential area of Palm Beach County. The U.S. transportation secretary says all nine people aboard died.
Turning to the midterms, two days before Election Day.
In Michigan, Haley Stevens, who lost the Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers. President Trump called it, quote, "a big move," end quote. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it a, quote, "despicable betrayal," end quote.
In Alaska, a judge dismissed a Justice Department lawsuit seeking to delay certification of the Senate race. The case concerned two candidates named Dan Sullivan on the ballot. The Justice Department says it will appeal.
President Trump plans an event at the Reflecting Pool in Washington on the last day of the campaign. He maintains the pool was vandalized.
Connecticut Senator Chris Murphy, a Democrat, suffered a stroke and remains hospitalized. His chief of staff says he is, quote, "awake and alert," end quote.
And police in Dallas say four people were killed and 15 hurt in separate shootings on Halloween night. It was among several violent incidents across the country over the holiday weekend.
