## World: Iran and the Gulf

Talks between the United States and Iran have made no progress, officials on both sides say.
President Trump told CNN new strikes are, quote, "highly unlikely," end quote, if a strong deal is reached. He said the United States has identified targets, including 38 bridges.
Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says there has been no progress and that Iran is waiting until after the midterm elections.
Secretary of State Marco Rubio, arriving in France, called the talks, quote, "not very productive," end quote. He said Iran's demands are, quote, "outrageous and ridiculous," end quote, and that the United States will defend its forces and shipping.
Iran's Revolutionary Guard dismissed a reported U.S. plan to extend the blockade to land borders as, quote, "a play on words," end quote.
Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz says the mission against Iran is not complete.
Iranian media reported four blasts on Qeshm Island this evening. Tasnim news agency says a U.S. projectile hit the island. That has not been confirmed by the United States. Earlier, Iranian state television suggested some of the blasts were Iranian military activity.
Separately, Iranian state media say blasts in the port city of Mahshahr were two exploding electrical transformers, not an attack.
In the Strait of Hormuz, the Revolutionary Guard Navy claims three tankers caught fire on what it calls an unauthorized channel. That claim is unverified. The U.K. maritime agency reports a tanker fire northwest of Khasab, and its crew was evacuated.
In Iraq, a drone struck a gas field at Khor Mor in the Kurdistan region. Fire halted production, and about 2.8 gigawatts of power were lost. The prime minister says there were no casualties. No one has claimed responsibility.
Iraq's foreign minister says the U.S. Treasury asked Iraq to close all but two crossings with Iran. Another Iraqi minister says Iraq reached understandings with the United States to allow oil exports through the Strait of Hormuz. Details have not been given.

## World: Saudi Arabia and Yemen

Sources: AFP https://www.afp.com/en/yemens-houthis-claim-attack-riyadh-financial-district-aramco-skyscraper-damaged ; AP https://apnews.com/article/mideast-news-november-8-2026-090d96e25872f5c63e105b9dd3e5dc60 ; BBC https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6ly3mjx958eo ; Reuters (Yemen) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2119220873466880465 ; Al Jazeera (UNICEF) https://x.com/AJEnglish/status/2119409583005274280
In Saudi Arabia, the Houthis claim an attack today with missiles and drones on Riyadh's financial district, the airport, Aramco storage and Abqaiq, Agence France-Presse reports.
The Saudi Energy Ministry calls it an attempted attack. It says falling debris caused minor damage to an Aramco administrative building and no one was hurt. The coalition says it intercepted missiles fired toward Riyadh.
Witnesses told Agence France-Presse they saw shattered glass and facade damage on several floors. BBC Verify says authenticated footage shows more extensive damage than the ministry described, with at least one floor badly hit.
In Yemen, the Houthi-run health ministry says the toll from airstrikes near Sanaa is 33. Reuters says that figure is unverified. UNICEF says more than 300,000 people have been displaced.

## World: Ukraine and Russia

Sources: RFE/RL https://www.rferl.org/a/russia-strikes-ukraine-energy/33893219.html ; AFP https://x.com/AFP/status/2119413634975646197 ; IAEA https://x.com/iaeaorg/status/2119504174429704501 ; Straits Times (Bloomberg) https://www.straitstimes.com/world/europe/hacked-records-show-400000-russians-killed-in-war-on-ukraine ; Reuters (G7) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2119540483768660328
In Ukraine, more than 600,000 people are without power in the Dnipro region after overnight strikes on energy facilities, according to Radio Free Europe.
Ukraine says Russia launched 3 missiles and 164 drones, and that 137 were intercepted. One person was killed in Dnipro.
Agence France-Presse reports a new drone struck an aid center in Komyshuvakha, where rescuers were still recovering bodies.
The Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant lost all outside power early today, for the 27th time. It ran on diesel backup. The International Atomic Energy Agency says power was later restored.
Bloomberg reports hacked Russian government death-certificate data show about 392,700 deaths linked to the war through late August. Bloomberg says the authenticity of the data has not been cryptographically proven. Russia's statistics agency declined to comment.
G7 foreign ministers meet in Paris Monday and Tuesday. Italy's foreign minister says they will discuss suspected pro-Russian cyberattacks.

In Egypt, two passenger trains collided today in Ayyat, in Giza province, about 32 miles south of Cairo. The health ministry says 7 people were killed, including a train driver, and 112 were injured.
A Giza to Fayoum train struck the rear of a Cairo to Aswan service. President Sisi ordered an investigation, and prosecutors opened an inquiry. The cause is not yet clear.
In Poland, two days of national mourning began for the six people killed in Saturday's shooting in Krakow. The national prosecutor opened a terrorism investigation. Police confirmed that three bodies found at the suspect's home were his father, mother and sister.
In South Africa, the African National Congress won 30.76% in local elections, its weakest result since apartheid. The Democratic Alliance had 24.64%. President Cyril Ramaphosa accepted the result, the BBC reports.
In Israel, President Isaac Herzog formally tasked Gadi Eisenkot with forming the next government. Eisenkot has 28 days and called for a national emergency government.
In Colombia, a drone attack on an army base killed 3 soldiers and wounded 16. In Senegal, the government says at least 21 people died when a migrant boat sank.
On Spain's Canary Islands, El Hierro declared an emergency after more than 3,300 migrant arrivals since November 1st.
In Japan, a magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck western Shizuoka Saturday. Kyodo and NHK report at least 20 people were injured in Aichi, mostly in falls. There was no tsunami.
In Germany, Chancellor Friedrich Merz says he intends to nominate Ilse Aigner for president. She would be the country's first woman in the office.

Here at home, Uwchlan Township supervisors denied a 492-home rezoning on November 4th. Industrial zoning stays in place. MyChesCo reports the developer expects to return within 60 days under current zoning.
In Harrisburg, House Bill 2888 would require large data centers, 20 megawatts or more, to secure power and disclose water and energy use. It also sets investment and jobs commitments. It keeps local zoning authority. The bill is in the House Energy Committee, and the session ends November 30th.
Chester County has 2 new measles cases in two weeks, for a county total of 94, MyChesCo reports. Statewide, 6 deaths have been reported.
Honey Brook Township supervisors will consider a $52 annual local services tax Wednesday at 7 P.M. It would apply to people who work in the township, including the self-employed and nonresidents. It has not been adopted.
PennDOT will close lanes in both directions on U.S. 1 in Kennett and East Marlborough Townships, around the clock, from Monday through November 20th, weather permitting.
In Philadelphia, police separated groups in Rittenhouse Square when a ride targeting Jewish-owned restaurants met a large counter-demonstration. No arrests or injuries were reported.
The forecast is dry through midweek. The National Weather Service says a coastal system is likely late in the week, and the chance of snow is highly uncertain.
