In Ukraine tonight, Russian ballistic missiles struck the capital, Kyiv.
Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko says a shopping center, an industrial building and another non-residential building were hit in the Desnianskyi district.
Ukrainian media identify the shopping center as the Prospekt mall.
Casualty reports vary. Mr. Klitschko reported 3 people injured, one seriously. The Kyiv Post later reported 4 injured, 2 of them in serious condition. Those figures are preliminary.
Earlier, Russia launched drones at energy and civilian targets across Ukraine overnight.
Euronews, citing Ukrainian authorities, reports at least 7 people were killed in 6 regions.
The Ukrainian energy company Naftogaz says a gas production site in the Poltava region was hit and caught fire. The site has suspended operations.
Ukraine, for its part, says its drones struck 3 Russian oil refineries overnight, 2 in the Samara region and 1 in the Rostov region.
Ukraine's General Staff also says it struck Savasleyka air base, about 185 miles east of Moscow. It says 3 MiG-31 jets and 2 Su-34 bombers were damaged.
Russia's Defense Ministry says it shot down drones over the region, but it did not mention the base. The damage has not been independently confirmed.

## Second Amendment cases
<!-- summary: Supreme Court of Virginia rules the state's felon gun ban unconstitutional as applied to a nonviolent offender; the law was not struck down generally. -->
Sources: Supreme Court of Virginia (opinion) https://www.vacourts.gov/static/opinions/opnscvwp/1250051.pdf ; Courthouse News (Ginevan) https://www.courthousenews.com/virginia-supreme-court-rules-state-unconstitutionally-disarmed-nonviolent-felon/
In Second Amendment cases, the Supreme Court of Virginia ruled today in Ginevan versus Commonwealth.
The court unanimously reversed a conviction under Virginia's ban on felons possessing firearms. It held the ban unconstitutional as applied to Derek Ginevan, whose record includes drug and larceny offenses.
The ruling applies to this case only. The law itself was not struck down.
Justice Wesley Russell wrote that the state did not offer one founding-era law that permanently disarmed felons.
The state had defended the ban as consistent with the nation's tradition of disarming felons. The attorney general's office did not comment.
Federal law still bars felons from possessing firearms. The Supreme Court in Washington hears its next major gun case, on semiautomatic rifle bans, December 2nd.

For your business: the new tariffs are now formally proposed.
The U.S. Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, determined that practices tied to excess manufacturing capacity in 14 economies burden American commerce. They include China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam and India.
Mr. Greer told CNBC most rates range from 15 to 20%, and generally will not stack on top of existing duties. He called the effect, quote, a top up, end quote.
China faces an added 20%, on top of tariffs of about 25% from 2018. A senior official says that brings tariffs on many Chinese goods to about 45%.
China's foreign ministry says the so-called overcapacity, quote, does not really exist, end quote.
Public comments are due January 5th, and hearings begin January 25th. Mr. Greer says none of the tariffs would take effect for at least a couple of months.
