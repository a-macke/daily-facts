President Trump said today that Iran wants to make a deal, and that a deal, quote, "could happen," end quote.

In Ukraine, officials say Russian aerial bombs killed at least 26 people in the village of Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region. The figures are preliminary.
President Zelenskyy says the first strike hit a school used to hand out humanitarian aid every Monday. He says a second strike then hit a shelter where people had taken cover. He called it, quote, "further proof that Russia wants no de-escalation," end quote.
Ukraine's emergency service says the dead include two rescue workers and four police officers. The regional governor, Mykhailo Semikin, says 27 people were reported missing.
The strike came a day after a local truce around the occupied town of Oleshky, in the Kherson region, expired. Kyiv says Russia has resumed strikes on Kherson city, and that Russia used the truce to rearm.
Both countries say their public alert apps were hacked and sent false warnings. Ukrainian officials say fake nuclear warnings were among them.
Bloomberg reports Ukraine resumed drone strikes on Russian oil sites. It also reports, citing people familiar, that the United States will ask NATO allies for more money for a Ukraine weapons program.

Across Europe, a cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration hit stores in at least seven countries overnight, according to the BBC and Reuters. Affected chains include Delhaize, Carrefour and Colruyt in Belgium, ICA and Coop in Sweden, and SuperValu in Ireland. Stores also reported problems in the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Finland and Estonia.
A Belgian refrigeration firm says the attackers switched units to defrost mode, warming them. Stores threw away spoiled food. Belgian prosecutors opened a criminal investigation.
The European Union called it, quote, "a deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain," end quote. No one has claimed responsibility. Reuters says it could not establish who was behind the attack. Moscow has repeatedly denied involvement in sabotage in Europe.
It follows a similar attack on French supermarkets three weeks ago.
In Bulgaria, Iliana Iotova was elected president, the first woman to hold the post, Reuters reports.
In the Democratic Republic of Congo, Bloomberg reports the death rate among confirmed Ebola cases has passed 50%.
In Gaza, medics told Reuters Israeli strikes killed three people today.
In Yemen, Reuters, citing sources, reports Houthi forces are within 80 kilometers of Aden.
And CBS News reports four Americans were among the 69 people killed in a train crash in Spain.

On the eve of the midterm elections, the Supreme Court rejected a Trump administration request to make California set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 people the administration says are likely noncitizens.
The order was one line and unsigned. There were no noted dissents.
The Justice Department argues federal law bars noncitizens from voting, and that once ballots are mixed, they cannot be pulled back. California says noncitizen voting is already illegal under state law. A federal judge had called the government's list, quote, "rife with uncertainty and errors," end quote. An appeals court also ruled against the administration.
Separately, Bloomberg reports a federal judge is weighing whether to block the Justice Department from sending federal law enforcement officers as poll monitors.
AdImpact estimates about $11.6B in ad spending this cycle, the most expensive midterm on record, Al Jazeera reports.
President Trump made his closing argument at the Reflecting Pool in Washington today. Polls open tomorrow.
In Florida, the National Transportation Safety Board says early evidence shows the Learjet 60 that crashed in Palm Beach County began breaking apart before impact. All nine people aboard died. CBS News reports a pilot was heard saying, quote, "we're managing the ice," end quote, before a mayday call. The F.A.A. says the jet was registered to a company in Wilmington, Delaware.
