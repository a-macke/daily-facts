Turning to the Middle East.
A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Iran has sent the United States a nuclear proposal through Qatar. The official said it would be discussed only after Washington meets seven Iranian demands. The official said Washington is reviewing it. The contents have not been disclosed.
Iran's demands include lifting sanctions, releasing frozen assets and ending the American naval blockade. Washington has said nuclear issues must be part of any first negotiations.
The New York Times reports Iran is reviewing what it calls concrete proposals from mediators.
At sea, Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two tankers and a tugboat caught fire near the Strait of Hormuz, after taking what it called an unauthorized route. Iran's navy says one tanker hit a mine. Those accounts have not been independently confirmed.
Saudi Arabia warned of danger at its Yanbu oil hub, amid Houthi attacks, Reuters reports.

Here at home, a contractor struck an underground fuel pipeline this afternoon on River Road in Upper Merion, near Bridgeport.
NBC10 reports the pipeline belongs to Sunoco, and was hit during excavation for a new electric tower. NBC10 reports the leaked fuel was aviation fuel. Other outlets described it as gasoline.
Estimates of the amount differ. Officials told NBC10 thousands of gallons spilled. The Philadelphia Inquirer reports, citing a township official, that Sunoco estimated hundreds of thousands of gallons leaked.
Upper Merion's public safety director told NBC10 the pipeline was shut down quickly. He said the spill was contained to the site and an adjacent quarry.
A shelter-in-place order was lifted at about 5:20 this evening. Upper Merion police say there is no immediate threat to the public.
The Inquirer reports River Road remains closed near Coates Street, with no timeline to reopen. NBC10 reported parts of Route 23 were beginning to reopen at about 6.
In Philadelphia, District Attorney Larry Krasner announced murder charges in the death of 10-year-old Malayshia Alford. Police had earlier identified her as Jalayshia.
Her foster mother, Caprice Williams, 36, is charged with murder, abuse of a corpse and related offenses.
Investigators say the girl's body was found Wednesday night in the trunk of a car in Lindenwold, New Jersey. They say an autopsy found she died of blunt force trauma. Prosecutors say she was last seen alive on October 22nd.
In Harrisburg, the Inquirer reports Governor Shapiro will push to raise the minimum wage with the new Democratic majority.
SEPTA reports serious crime on its system fell 10%.
In Chester County, the Tredyffrin-Easttown school board has set a vote on school boundary changes.
And the rain today ended an 18-day dry spell in Philadelphia. Heavier rain is expected into Friday.

Reuters reports American tariffs on Canadian kitchen cabinets are set to double, to 50%, on January 1st.
The head of Cabico, one of Canada's largest cabinet makers, told Reuters he is weighing production in the United States. He said the company's shipments to the U.S. have fallen 25% from 2024 levels.
The president of the Canadian Kitchen Cabinet Association said many companies would close if the rate doubles.
Bloomberg reports the Justice Department has a grand jury investigating pricing among large American cardboard box makers. Packaging Dive reports Smurfit Westrock, International Paper and Packaging Corporation of America received subpoenas. The companies say they are cooperating.
And the federal government warned of an ongoing cyber threat to industrial refrigeration and cold storage systems, Reuters reports.
