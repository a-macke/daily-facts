Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive and board member of Nvidia, effective immediately, Reuters reports.
Chief financial officer Colette Kress has been named interim chief executive, CNBC reports. Former Synopsys chief executive Harvey Jones was named the company's first board chair.
Bloomberg reports Mr. Huang was ousted after reports of two relationships. Bloomberg says one woman was suspected by US officials of spying for China. That claim has not been confirmed.
Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department has not concluded the woman was a spy. He said the FBI is investigating. Beijing denied the allegation, AFP reports.
Reuters, citing sources, reports Mr. Huang also had a relationship with a second employee.
Nvidia shares fell about 14% at the open. Its market value dropped below $4 trillion. Bloomberg reports Mr. Huang forfeits about $108M in unvested shares.
Representative Brian Mast says Mr. Huang agreed to testify to Congress before Thanksgiving, likely behind closed doors.
The New York Times reports President Trump urged Mr. Huang in a call on Tuesday to keep fighting for his job. Aides say the president saw Nvidia as essentially too big to fail.
A Democratic senator is now asking for an assessment of whether Nvidia is too big to fail, Reuters reports. Reuters also reports tougher curbs on AI chip sales to China are likely after the resignation.
Separately, Reuters reports the White House is discussing the future of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.
