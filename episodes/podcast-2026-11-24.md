In the Persian Gulf, Iran's navy says 2 more oil tankers struck naval mines east of the Strait of Hormuz early today.
In a statement carried by state media, it says the ships had switched off their tracking systems. It says they caught fire and cannot move.
The Revolutionary Guard navy separately says a 4th tanker exploded after hitting a mine.
Iran did not name the ships or give details on their crews. None of the claims has been independently confirmed.
The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations center says 3 vessels were struck by unknown projectiles on Monday evening. It says they continued their voyages, and all crew members were accounted for.
ABC News notes U.S. Central Command has called earlier Iranian mine claims false.
Euronews reports Iran's army commander, Major General Amir Hatami, on Monday ordered the strait closed, quote, further day by day, end quote, until Iran's conditions are met.
Qatar's prime minister met Secretary of State Rubio and Vice President Vance in Washington on Monday. He says Qatar is working to bring both sides back to talks.
In Saudi Arabia, the civil defense agency says 11 people were injured in a Houthi attack on a tower in Riyadh's financial district. Bloomberg reports it is the second attack on the district in under a week.

For your business: new tariffs appear close.
Reuters and A.P., citing people familiar with the matter, report the administration will soon announce tariffs on China, the European Union and 14 other economies.
The duties would follow an investigation into what the administration calls excess factory capacity. They would partly replace tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February.
Reuters reports the rates would be tailored to each country. It says capped rates in existing trade deals, such as 15% for the E.U., Japan and South Korea, are expected to be honored.
China has rejected the accusations, saying its exports reflect competitive advantages. As of this evening, no announcement had been made.
Bloomberg reports cheaper Asian steel is gaining with American buyers despite tariffs, as data center construction squeezes domestic supply and raises prices.
And China has extended its pause on export curbs to the U.S. for gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials, until January 27th.
