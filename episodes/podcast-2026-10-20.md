President Zelenskyy met US envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo today. Jared Kushner joined by video link.
The leaders of Norway and Finland also took part, the Norwegian government says.
A US official, speaking to Reuters on condition of anonymity, called the talks "constructive."
The official said they covered security guarantees, a postwar economic plan, and European security.
Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre says Witkoff will fly to Saint Petersburg on Wednesday to meet Russian leaders.

Kyiv had accused Washington of withholding intelligence and delaying interceptor missiles.
Secretary of State Marco Rubio called that accusation "scandalous," the BBC reports. Washington denies any deliberate delay.
In the Oval Office, President Trump said, quote, "Ukraine has to want to settle this war," end quote.
On the ground, ABC Australia reports Russian strikes killed at least seven people across Ukraine on Tuesday.
Reuters reports Russia has extended its strikes on energy sites to western Ukraine.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, says the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until Washington accepts Tehran's conditions.
He told Iraqi television that indirect talks continue through Pakistan and Qatar.
He says the Americans agreed to Iran's terms but wanted to add provisions. The United States has not commented on that account.
Earlier, Reuters reported Iran told Pakistan's army chief there would be no talks with Washington until its demands are met.
A Qatari delegation arrived in Tehran this evening, Iranian media say, according to Reuters.
An Arabic-language outlet, citing unnamed sources, reported a Qatari and Pakistani compromise proposal. It has not been officially confirmed. Bloomberg quotes a diplomat who disputed that a deal is close.
In Israel, Reuters reports Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth made an unannounced visit and discussed Iran with Prime Minister Netanyahu.
Netanyahu said today, quote, "Finishing the job means bringing down the terrorist regime in Iran," end quote. Iran International notes Washington has not publicly committed to that goal.
Israelis vote on October 27th.

In Yemen, Reuters reports the toll from Monday's Houthi missile attack on a training camp in Shabwa has risen to 73.
Authorities say more than 200 were wounded and 14 are missing. The figures are preliminary.
Britain is sending Typhoon jets and military advisers to Saudi Arabia, Al Jazeera reports.
An Iraqi official says the prime minister ordered the Sheeb border crossing with Iran closed indefinitely. No reason was given.
And Egypt's parliament has postponed its vote on sending forces to Saudi Arabia until October 26th.

## World: Africa
Sources: France 24 / AP (Abyei toll 153) https://www.france24.com/en/africa/20261020-more-than-150-killed-sudan-drone-strike-disputed-abyei-border-region ; Eye Radio (toll above 220) https://eyeradio.org/death-toll-from-abyei-market-drone-strike-rises-to-more-than-220/ ; BBC (Sudanese military source) https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cr3el70jz1nqo ; Reuters (Mali) https://x.com/Reuters/status/2112540632317096049 ; Bloomberg (Nigeria Ebola) https://x.com/business/status/2112675270033383756 ; Al Jazeera (Ebola contacts) https://x.com/AJEnglish/status/2112690325844005043
In the disputed Abyei region, on the Sudan and South Sudan border, the toll from Monday's drone strike on a market keeps rising.
Local officials first reported 73 dead. On Tuesday, they told the Associated Press 153.
Late Tuesday, the area's information minister told Eye Radio more than 220 had been killed. All figures are preliminary.
Local officials blame Sudan's army.
A Sudanese military source told the BBC the army carried out the strike. The source described the market as a fuel supply site for the Rapid Support Forces.
A witness told reporters he saw no fighters in the market.
In Mali, Reuters sources say Islamic State-linked militants killed at least 48 civilians near Menaka over the weekend. That has not been officially confirmed.
And Nigeria has reported its first Ebola case in the current outbreak, a traveler from Uganda. The health minister says the patient is stable, and 44 contacts are being traced.

And for your business. The Commerce Department has cut duties on most Canadian softwood lumber to 24.75%, from about 35%.
The new rates were published in the Federal Register on October 19th.
A separate 10% national security tariff still applies. That brings the total to 34.75%, according to the home builders' association, which still urges suspending the tariffs.
Framing lumber averaged about $499 per thousand board feet on October 16th, down 4.6% from a year ago, the association reports.
The Lumber Daily reports producer prices for hardwood lumber fell 0.5% in September. Softwood plywood rose 2.7%, its 11th straight monthly gain.
Reuters reports single-family housing starts fell sharply, and permits hit a three-and-a-half-year low.
Bloomberg reports pending home sales fell 7.3% in September.
