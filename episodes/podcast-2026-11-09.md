President Trump met at the White House today with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani. Qatar has been the main go-between with Iran.
The prime minister also met separately with Vice President JD Vance. He called the talks positive and said they focused on advancing negotiations between the United States and Iran.
Afterward, Mr. Trump made an unscheduled appearance in the White House briefing room. The Associated Press reports he said, quote, "Iran militarily is effectively over," end quote.
Mr. Trump told Reuters he will hold off on new strikes for now, at the request of Iran's neighbors. He said the naval blockade will stay in place. He told reporters the United States is, quote, "fully locked and loaded," end quote, but does not expect to need its weapons.
Axios reports mediators are discussing a two-day plan. The United States would lift its blockade and release some frozen Iranian funds. Iran would invite nuclear inspectors and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Washington has not accepted that plan.
Iran's account differs. Mohsen Rezaei, the head of Iran's security council, said on social media that the United States should, quote, "stop manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks," end quote. He said Washington should accept Iran's seven conditions.
Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Tehran is ready to negotiate an end to the conflict, but will not yield to pressure.
Turkey's intelligence chief said Qatar will host Vice President Vance and Mr. Rezaei next week. That meeting has not been confirmed by Washington or Tehran.
In Saudi Arabia, state media say the East-West oil pipeline was hit today in the Medina region. Earlier, the Saudi Press Agency said the pipeline caught fire, but operations were not affected.
Near the Strait of Hormuz, a tanker caught fire after Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed it struck three tankers on Sunday. That claim has not been independently verified.
In Paris, French officials accuse Iran's Revolutionary Guard of cyberattacks last month that knocked out commercial refrigerators and freezers across France. Reuters reports the officials say there will be consequences.
In markets, Brent crude fell 5.9% to $92.57 a barrel. The S and P 500 rose about 2.3%, its biggest gain since April, the Associated Press reports.

In Washington, Republican Congressman Thomas Massie of Kentucky filed eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.
Three articles charge that the war with Iran violates the War Powers Resolution. Others concern strikes on alleged drug boats, the capture of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro, action against Senator Mark Kelly, and military action in Yemen.
Because the measure is privileged, the House must take it up within two legislative days. Republican leaders could move to set it aside.
Mr. Hegseth says he is ready to defend his record. President Trump said, quote, "What has Hegseth done? He won the war," end quote. The Washington Post notes conviction in the Senate would face long odds.
Bloomberg reports some Democrats are weighing the December 11th government funding deadline as leverage to press for an end to the Iran war.

In artificial intelligence news, Nvidia shares closed down 4.3% at $178.67. That cut the company's market value by about $190 billion, Reuters reports.
It was the first trading day since Nvidia's board opened an investigation into chief executive Jensen Huang. News reports say U.S. authorities concluded a woman he had an affair with, Alice Huang, is a Chinese intelligence asset. She is not related to him.
Mr. Huang told employees he was unfaithful to his wife and says he will cooperate fully. China's foreign ministry calls the spy claims unsubstantiated.
Shareholders filed a proposed class action in federal court in San Jose, minutes after the market closed. It accuses Nvidia and Mr. Huang of misleading investors. Nvidia did not immediately comment.
President Trump says the F.B.I. is looking into the matter. He says Mr. Huang told him he had no idea.
Anthropic released new models, Mythos 5.2 and Fable 5.2, at prices about 20% below the previous versions.
