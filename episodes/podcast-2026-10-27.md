Israelis voted today in the first national election since the October 7th attack.
Turnout was 77.7%, the highest since 1999. Israel's Central Elections Committee says about 5.7 million people voted, Al Jazeera reports.
Exit polls show no bloc with a majority in the 120-seat Knesset.
The latest Israeli media projections give Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc 51 seats, and the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot 51 seats. Arab-led parties are projected to win 18.
AFP says the initial projections give the opposition a narrow edge. Reuters and CBS describe the exit polls as pointing to a deadlock.
Mr. Eisenkot says Israelis voted for change. He vowed to form a government for all citizens, and warned against another election.
Naftali Bennett says Mr. Netanyahu has lost.
Mr. Netanyahu warned against an opposition government backed by Arab parties, Israeli media report. Arab party leaders called that anti-Arab rhetoric.
Gideon Saar says celebrations are premature.
Bezalel Smotrich said he would, quote, "not let," end quote, Arab citizens celebrate. Israeli media report Itamar Ben Gvir's party lost seats.
During the day, Likud and Shas petitioned to extend voting hours. Mr. Eisenkot's party opposed the request. It accused Mr. Netanyahu of laying groundwork to challenge the results, and cited reports of alleged ballot tampering. Those allegations have not been independently confirmed.
Israeli state radio says official results are expected by Wednesday evening.
