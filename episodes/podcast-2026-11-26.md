In Ukraine, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Russia is preparing another mass strike in the coming days.
In his nightly address, he said Ukrainian intelligence shows Moscow is completing preparations. He said the targets would again be energy, logistics and other vital services.
He asked Ukrainians to pay close attention to missile alerts. It was his second such warning this week.
In Kharkiv today, Russian drone strikes killed 3 people, Ukrainian officials say. Regional governor Oleh Syniehubov says one of them was an 86-year-old woman, killed when a drone hit a university building.
Ukraine's Air Force says Russia launched ballistic missiles and 113 drones overnight. It says 87 drones were shot down or neutralized.
In Kyiv, Mayor Vitali Klitschko now says 7 people were injured in last night's missile strike on a shopping center. Moscow said it struck drone-related sites in the city.

In the Persian Gulf tonight, 2 Italian technicians were killed today in a drone attack in Kuwait.
Italy's foreign ministry says the men worked for the Italian unit of the German defense company Rheinmetall. They were maintaining antennas for an air defense system used by Kuwait's armed forces.
Kuwait's Defense Ministry says Iranian drones targeted a vital site and were intercepted. It says falling debris wounded 4 people, including the 2 Italians, who later died.
Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, told the Senate in Rome the site was targeted by Iranian drones. He said Italy would lodge a complaint with Iran's embassy.
Iran denies any role. An armed forces spokesman said Iran carried out no military operations in the region. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran was prepared to investigate jointly with Kuwait to find the source.
In Saudi Arabia, a projectile struck the Jazan oil refinery on the Red Sea coast. The Saudi Energy Ministry says a Nepali worker was killed and several people were injured. A fire was put out, and some operations were temporarily halted.
The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen says the Houthis carried out the attack. It says it then destroyed the missile launch platform used, in Yemen's Hodeidah province. There was no immediate statement from the Houthis on that claim.

Closer to home tonight, a Thanksgiving nor'easter brought heavy rain and damaging wind to Southeast Pennsylvania.
PECO reported more than 81,000 customers without power at 5:30 this evening. Chester County was hit hardest, with almost 44,000 customers out, or about 1 in 5. Delaware County had about 21,000.
A PECO spokesman, Greg Smore, says crews are working around the clock. PECO has not posted a restoration estimate.
The National Weather Service has a High Wind Warning for Chester County until 1 a.m. Friday. The weekend is forecast to be dry.
SEPTA suspended service on several Regional Rail lines during the storm, including the Media and Chestnut Hill West lines.
The Delaware River Port Authority says the Commodore Barry Bridge will stay closed until Saturday. It says high winds badly damaged work platforms on the bridge this morning.
In Lancaster County, Lancaster Farm Fresh Cooperative has voluntarily recalled its turkeys after customers reported a sulfur smell, the Philadelphia Inquirer reports.
In Royersford, borough council plans to vote January 12th on a revised zoning ordinance for data centers. No data center is proposed in the borough.
And Pennsylvania health officials report 41 new measles cases since Monday. That brings the state total to 1,679 in 46 counties, with 9 deaths.

In artificial intelligence news, the Financial Times reports Nvidia has told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia.
The paper cites 3 people familiar with the matter. It follows new U.S. rules requiring export licenses for advanced chips sent to several Southeast Asian countries. It is not clear whether the halt is temporary. Nvidia did not immediately comment.
In Delaware, a group of 42 state attorneys general is asking a bankruptcy court to block the sale of user chat histories by Character.AI, a chatbot company.
The attorneys general say the sale covers more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors. They say it has no safeguards for users. Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October.
And Bloomberg reports the German software maker SAP is considering at least 10,000 job cuts as part of a restructuring around artificial intelligence. SAP has not confirmed the report.
