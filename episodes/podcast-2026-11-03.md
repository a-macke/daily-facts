We begin in the Middle East.
U.S. officials told CBS News that American forces carried out what they called "limited defensive strikes" overnight, near the Strait of Hormuz and Qeshm Island.
President Trump today called the strikes, quote, "a minor attack," end quote. He said, quote, "not a war," end quote. Reuters reports the president told the Financial Times the United States has no immediate plan to strike Iran again.
A senior Iranian figure told CBS News the American explanation was, quote, "not convincing," end quote. Rezaei, a senior Iranian official, said the United States must answer Iran's demands before the strait fully reopens.
This evening, Iran said a plan for what it calls "maximum deterrence" is ready against any new American attack. Iran says its target lists now include U.S. and Israeli assets.
At sea, the British maritime agency U.K.M.T.O. says an oil tanker was struck by two unknown projectiles near Hormuz. Earlier, Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed three tankers exploded on mines. That claim has not been independently confirmed.
On diplomacy, Bloomberg reports mediators flew to Tehran for more talks. Qatar says partners are working to secure a deal.
Separately, President Trump says the United States will stop about $4 billion a year in military aid to Israel in 2027, while continuing to sell it arms. He says that is under an arrangement with Prime Minister Netanyahu. Israeli opposition leader Yair Lapid called the decision, quote, "one of the most severe political and security failures" in Israel's history, end quote.
