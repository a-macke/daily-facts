In the Persian Gulf region, Iran's Revolutionary Guards say 2 more oil tankers have struck naval mines. The claims have not been independently confirmed.
In a statement carried by Iran's state news agency, the Guards' navy said one tanker hit a mine at the entrance to Oman's port of Khasab. It said a second tanker exploded and caught fire east of the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards did not name the vessels.
The U.S. Central Command says American forces disabled a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday. It says the Palau-flagged Pacific Archer tried to run the U.S. naval blockade after leaving the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas.
Central Command says a U.S. aircraft struck the ship's stern with precision munitions after multiple warnings. It says the civilian crew was unharmed.
An Iranian Guards commander, Majid Mousavi, said the, quote, enemy, end quote, should expect action from the air and the sea.
Iran's navy chief says Iranian forces recovered 2 tankers he says were taken by the U.S. military. Central Command calls that account false. It also denies an Iranian claim that it charges ships a toll to pass through the strait.
In Kuwait, Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, says the drone that killed 2 Italian technicians on Thursday was made in Iran. He cited investigations by Kuwaiti authorities.
Iran denies launching the drone from its territory. It has offered to join an investigation with Italy and Kuwait. Kuwait has summoned Iran's envoy.
In Yemen, accounts conflict over 11 deaths. Authorities in Taiz say Houthi shelling killed 11 people in the town of al-Turbah. Houthi media say Saudi strikes killed 11 people. Neither account has been independently verified.

Turning to the war in Ukraine.
Reuters reports a convoy of Russian ships carrying military vehicles to Libya has turned around in the Mediterranean.
The news agency cites 3 people from military and security services. The convoy turned back after Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian navy corvette in the Libyan port of Benghazi on November 22nd. Russia has not commented on the report.
President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Ukraine coordinates its naval strikes with partners. He acknowledged the Libya strike was not coordinated with European partners. Italy denies any coordination.
Mr. Zelenskyy repeated his warning that Russia is preparing a new mass strike in the coming days, aimed at energy and logistics. No such attack had been reported by this evening.
In the city of Kremenchuk, a Russian drone attack injured 4 people, including 3 children, the regional governor says.
Ukraine has stopped exporting electricity, the Kyiv Independent reports, as Russian attacks strain the power grid.
The International Atomic Energy Agency says it is working with Russia and Ukraine on a local ceasefire. It would allow repairs to a power line serving the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant.

Closer to home, PECO says power should be back for most customers by Saturday, and for all customers by Sunday.
The utility calls the Thanksgiving storm the 20th most damaging in its history. At its peak, about 225,000 customers lost power.
On Friday morning, PECO reported about 34,000 customers still without service, most of them in Chester and Delaware Counties. PECO says crews from as far away as Tennessee and Canada are helping.
The Commodore Barry Bridge remained closed in both directions today. High winds damaged 3 work platforms suspended above the roadway.
In West Philadelphia, a boil-water advisory remains in effect for thousands of customers. NBC10 reported officials expect it to be resolved on Saturday.
A federal judge in Washington has blocked the Federal Communications Commission from an early review of the licenses of 8 ABC-owned stations, including 6abc in Philadelphia.
Judge Loren AliKhan wrote that FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, quote, has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders, end quote. ABC called the ruling a victory for the First Amendment. An FCC spokesperson said the decision was, quote, wrong on the facts and wrong on the law, end quote, and that the agency expects to prevail.
The Housing Authority of Chester County will lower its housing voucher payment standards on January 1st, from 110% to 100% of federal fair market rents. The change applies to new participants and to voucher holders who move. The authority cites rising costs.
In West Chester, the holiday event West Chester Lights Up is under way, with free parking downtown through Sunday.
The National Weather Service expects dry weather through Saturday. Rain arrives Sunday night with a cold front, about one-half to one inch, followed by gusty winds on Monday.

In artificial intelligence news, OpenAI has countersued Apple in federal court in California.
Apple sued OpenAI in June, accusing it and 2 former Apple employees of stealing trade secrets to develop hardware. OpenAI now claims Apple's employment practices let it accuse almost any departing worker, and the new employer, of theft.
Apple says the case is about OpenAI employees deliberately taking its confidential information. It calls the countersuit, quote, a transparent attempt to distract, end quote.
Shares of Anthropic rose about 11% today, to close at $91.88, Bloomberg reports. That is above its initial public offering price of $83.
Reuters reports hackers have breached the Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, called Cloud One. It cites 3 people familiar with the matter. NBC News reports a U.S. official confirmed the breach. It is not known whether any data was taken. The Air Force has not commented.
Bloomberg reports servers built on Nvidia's advanced Blackwell chips have turned up at a data center near Shanghai. U.S. rules require export licenses for such hardware. Bloomberg says the site is linked to a person named Alice Huang. Nvidia has not commented on the report.

A safety note for shops with fuel-burning heaters. The Consumer Product Safety Commission has announced a recall of about 210,000 Steadfast Haven plug-in carbon monoxide and natural gas detectors. Models K.H. one-fifty-eight and G.C. oh-three may fail to sound an alarm.
They were sold online from February through September of this year. The commission says to keep using them until a free replacement arrives.
