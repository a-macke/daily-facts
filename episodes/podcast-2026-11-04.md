Democrats will control both the House and the Senate when the new Congress convenes in January. That is according to projections reported this morning by Reuters, Agence France-Presse and CBS News.
In the Senate, Democrats won races in Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, New Hampshire, Michigan and Maine. In Maine, Democrat Jackson defeated Republican Senator Susan Collins.
In Texas, Democrat James Talarico won the Senate race over Republican Ken Paxton. CBS News reports Mr. Talarico called his win a message to Elon Musk. Mr. Musk spent more than $150M backing Mr. Paxton.
In Minnesota, Democrat Peggy Flanagan defeated Republican Tafoya.
In Michigan, Reuters reports Abdul El-Sayed will become the first Muslim United States senator.
Two Senate races remain uncalled. In Mississippi, Al Jazeera reports Democrat Scott Colom leads Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by about one hundredth of a percentage point. The race in Alaska is also not yet decided.
In the governors' races, Reuters reports Democrats flipped at least five Republican-held governorships. Gina Hinojosa won in Texas. Amy Acton won in Ohio, and Republican Vivek Ramaswamy conceded. Deb Haaland won in New Mexico. CBS News reports she will be the first Native American woman to serve as a governor. CNBC reports Republicans did not flip any Democratic-held seats.
In the House, Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek the post of minority leader, according to Reuters. Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries is poised to become the first Black Speaker of the House.
Al Jazeera notes Democrats will not have the two-thirds majorities needed to override a presidential veto.
Mr. Jeffries said the Democratic agenda will be, quote, "affordability and accountability," end quote, not impeachment. President Trump told CBS News he is, quote, "not even a little bit" concerned about impeachment, end quote. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said his party has a chance, quote, "to prove ourselves worthy," end quote.

President Trump today said the war with Iran was partly to blame for Republican losses. The Associated Press quotes him saying, quote, "we had to do" it, end quote. Al Jazeera reports he said the war likely contributed through higher gasoline prices, but that he would, quote, "do it again," end quote.
Mr. Trump said he is willing to work with Democrats. He also vowed to veto what he called "insane" legislation.
Vice President JD Vance said the Iran war cost Republicans the midterms. Reuters reports he posted that the party should ask whether it was worth the cost.
Florida Governor Ron DeSantis called the results, quote, "close to an extinction-level event" for Republicans, end quote.
CBS News earlier reported, citing unnamed sources, that the president was asking advisers whether Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth should stay. Mr. Trump later told CBS News he is not considering removing him. Democratic Representative Jason Crow of Colorado said Mr. Hegseth "needs to go."
Reuters reports Republican allies are pressing the president to change staff and to drop the Iran war and tariff policies. The White House has not confirmed any changes.
Senator Schumer said the war, quote, "must end," end quote.

On SEPTA, the Wilmington-Newark Line was suspended after a person was struck by an Amtrak train near Glenolden. Trolley tunnel service has resumed.
