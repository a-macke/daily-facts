Iranian negotiators met Qatari mediators in Doha today. A source briefed on the talks told Reuters the discussions are being coordinated with Washington. They follow more than 12 hours of talks in Tehran on Friday.
The two governments describe the contacts differently.
Secretary of State Marco Rubio says President Trump has not decided whether to launch new strikes, and would prefer a diplomatic solution. Rubio said, quote, "Talks have actually never stopped," end quote.
Iran's Foreign Minister, Abbas Araghchi, told Al Jazeera there are no plans to meet American officials. He called a letter from Vice President JD Vance a "positive step." He said whether talks are direct or indirect depends on Iran's conditions being met.
Araghchi says those conditions include lifting sanctions, reducing US forces in the region, and releasing frozen Iranian funds. He says a new proposal would shorten the time to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to two days.
At the same time, Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, issued a statement calling for an "immediate" and "geographically unrestricted" response to any threat. This evening, Al Jazeera reports he convened army commanders and urged them to upgrade capabilities and list Israeli and American targets.
Iran's navy, through state broadcaster IRIB, says a tanker that ignored warnings east of Hormuz hit mines and caught fire. That claim has not been confirmed. Separately, Iranian state media say the United States attacked fishing boats off Qeshm island. The United States has not confirmed that report.
In Yemen, a United Nations human rights staff member, Mohammed Ibrahim al-Shami, has died in Houthi custody. He had been detained since June of 2024. UN rights chief Volker Turk demands an independent investigation. Yemen's prime minister blames the Houthis. The UN humanitarian office says more than 290,000 people have been displaced in Yemen since August.

In Ukraine, President Volodymyr Zelensky says Russian strikes on energy sites left about 280,000 families without power. He says about 200,000 are in the Dnipro region, and more than 80,000 in the Kyiv region. Ukraine's air force says Russia launched 134 drones overnight, and 105 were downed.
Ukrainian drones struck a refinery complex deep inside Russia, Bloomberg reports. Russia's Deputy Prime Minister, Alexander Novak, says Ukraine attacked three refineries and a gas chemical complex, with minor damage at one plant.
The Wall Street Journal had reported that Ukraine agreed to pause refinery strikes until after the US midterms. Ukrainian officials told the Kyiv Independent that Kyiv never agreed to a pause.
From the occupied town of Oleshky, three planes of evacuees arrived in Belarus, Reuters reports. AFP says the evacuees were expected to reach Ukraine today. The White House says First Lady Melania Trump helped broker the local truce.
In Moscow, the US Embassy warned of a "credible threat of a mass casualty event" at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve. Russian state media say the FSB security service confirmed the American tip and took preventive measures. The site was closed. No attack has been reported.

Across Europe, young people marched in large Halloween protests.
In Germany, demonstrations were held in Berlin and more than 30 other cities. AFP reports police used water cannon and pepper spray after clashes. Police say some protesters threw fireworks, stones and bottles, and several officers were hurt.
In France, the government put turnout at about 320,000. Police used tear gas in Paris and Lyon. Tens of thousands also marched across Italy, Reuters reports.
In Poland, a dispute over the Constitutional Tribunal deepened. Judges backed by the government suspended the court's president.
President Karol Nawrocki cut short a trip to Africa. Reuters shows video of him forcing his way into the court building. Nawrocki accuses Prime Minister Donald Tusk's government of a forced takeover of the court.
Tusk defends the suspension. He says he is restoring the court's independence, and calls the president's entry an act of intimidation.

In Miami, a federal judge dismissed President Trump's defamation lawsuit against The Wall Street Journal and Rupert Murdoch. The suit sought $10B in damages. The suit concerned the paper's reporting on a birthday letter to Jeffrey Epstein that the paper says bore Trump's signature.
Judge Darrin Gayles ruled Trump did not plausibly show actual malice. He dismissed the case with prejudice, so it cannot be refiled in that court. The President denies writing the letter. A spokesperson for his legal team says he will, quote, "continue to press forward," end quote. A source told CBS News an appeal is likely.
The FBI says North Korea was behind this week's theft of more than $6B from the crypto exchange Binance. It names a North Korean unit known as TraderTraitor. Binance says it has replaced 90% of the stolen assets, and its customers will not bear the loss.
G20 foreign ministers ended their meeting in Atlanta without a consensus statement, CNBC reports. Secretary Rubio defended inviting Russia.
In energy markets, Reuters reports, citing sources, that OPEC plus has agreed in principle to keep December oil output targets steady.
And in California, one person was killed and six were hurt in a shooting at a Spirit Halloween store. Police are searching for the gunman.
